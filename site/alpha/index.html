Welcome

mailgoose is a deliberately small email marketing app. It does three things: you design an email, send it to people who asked to hear from you, and collect new subscribers with signup forms. Everything the web app does, the mailgoose CLI does too, so you can hand the work to whichever AI agent you already use.

Alpha means it's early. You'll be using our staging environment, not the finished product. Expect rough edges, things that change from one day to the next, and the odd outright bug. That's the point: every bug you find now is one a customer never sees.

  • The app: app.staging.mailgoose.app
  • The API, for the CLI: https://api.staging.mailgoose.app
  • Your data may be wiped. Staging is a test environment. Don't keep anything there you can't recreate.

You don't need to test everything. Pick the areas you'd actually use, follow their steps, and tell us whatever surprised, confused or annoyed you. "This button label made no sense" is as useful to us as a crash.

Get your account

  1. Sign up at app.staging.mailgoose.app/signup with the email address your invite went to. We email you a six-digit code; type it in and you're signed in. There are no passwords.
  2. Wait for review. Every new account is reviewed by hand, so you'll see a yellow banner saying so. Meanwhile you can build emails, import contacts and send yourself tests. Launching campaigns and taking form signups open once the review is done.
  3. Get your tester account. Once we've approved you, we turn your account into a tester account. A banner across the app then shows how many tester credits you have left and when your tester account ends.

If a day goes by and your account is still in review, say so in #user-feedback in the team Slack and we'll sort it out.

What a tester account can do

A tester account sends real campaigns to real people (people who agreed to get them), within some limits that keep staging safe for everyone:

LimitWhat it means
500 creditsFor the life of the tester account. Each campaign copy and each test send uses one. A copy that fails or is skipped gives its credit back.
30 daysAfter that the account can't test or launch. Ask us if you need longer.
500 contactsAdding or importing stops there.
100 a dayAt most 100 campaign copies in a day.
50 tests a dayTest sends, in any 24 hours.
A stricter pauseTwo spam complaints on one campaign, or a high hard-bounce rate, pauses the campaign and the account. We look, and unpause it if it was a false alarm.

Free practice addresses. Sends to Amazon's mailbox simulator, such as success@simulator.amazonses.com, use no tester credits and never count against your bounce or complaint rates. They're the safe way to see what a bounce or a complaint does. See Reports, bounces and unsubscribes.

Test mode, if you see it

New accounts start in test mode until we switch them over. In test mode, campaigns, tests and form signups reach only your own sign-in address and up to five test addresses you've confirmed. Add them on the Test addresses page (linked from the test mode notice) or with mailgoose test-addresses add; each one gets a link to click. A launch to anyone else is refused with test_mode. That's expected, not a bug.

Quick start: your first campaign

The Dashboard has the same five steps as a checklist, and ticks each one off as you go. Budget about 15 minutes.

  1. Fill in Settings.Your postal address (every email needs one by law) and your default From name and address.
  2. Sort out where your email comes from.Verify a domain of your own, or confirm a sender address. See Where your email comes from.
  3. Add a few contacts.Yourself, a second address of yours, and a simulator address or two.
  4. Build an emailwith blocks, or paste your own HTML.
  5. Make a campaign, send a test, then launch it.Check the test in your inbox first. Then open the report.

Area 1

Signing in

Where: the sign-in page, and mailgoose login

mailgoose has no passwords. You sign in with a six-digit code we email you, and you stay signed in for 30 days. The CLI signs in through the browser: it shows a short code, and you approve it on a page in the app.

How to test it

  1. Sign out (under your address in the side menu), then sign in again.
  2. Sign in on a second browser or your phone at the same time.
  3. Open the app in a private window and go straight to a page like /campaigns.

What good looks like

  • The code arrives within a minute, works once, and the email clearly comes from mailgoose.
  • A private window sends you to sign in first, then back to the page you asked for.

Edge cases worth poking at

  • A wrong code, a code typed with spaces, and an old code after you've asked for a new one.
  • Five wrong codes in a row (it should stop accepting that code).
  • Signing in with an address that has no account: you'll see the same message as for a real one, but no email. That's on purpose, so the form can't be used to find out who has an account.

Area 2

Settings

Where: Settings in the side menu, and mailgoose account get / account update

Your account's name, the default From name and address for new emails, the postal address that goes in every email's footer, your time zone (scheduled sends use it), dark mode, and deleting the account.

How to test it

  1. Fill in every field and save. Reload and check it all stuck.
  2. Turn on Dark mode under Appearance. Walk through every page and the email designer looking for text you can't read.
  3. Change your time zone, then schedule a campaign and check the time it shows.
  4. Run mailgoose account get and check it matches the page.

What good looks like

  • Your postal address shows in the footer of every test email you send.
  • Dark mode applies everywhere at once, other open tabs follow it, and it stays on after a reload. It's kept per browser, so a second browser starts light.

Edge cases worth poking at

  • A very long account name, accents and emoji, and a postal address with many lines.
  • Removing the postal address, then trying to send a test (it should refuse, and say why).
  • Deleting the account asks you to type its exact name. Please only try this on a second, throwaway account: deletion is immediate and can't be undone.

Area 3

Where your email comes from

Where: Domains, Settings, and mailgoose domain / sender-addresses

Every email needs a From address that you've proved is yours. There are two ways to do that.

  • Your own domain (recommended). Add a domain on the Domains page and put the DNS records it shows at your DNS host. mailgoose checks them every few minutes and marks the domain verified once they're all found. Then you can send from any address at that domain.
  • A sender address on mailgoose's shared domain. No DNS needed: you confirm an address you own (your sign-in address counts already) and your email goes from your account's address on mailgoose's shared sending domain, with replies to you. Rolling out on staging

Why we push you toward your own domain. Inbox providers judge mail partly by the domain it comes from. On your own domain you build your own reputation, your readers see your name, and nobody else's sending can drag you down. On a shared domain you share all of that with every other sender. The app nudges you about this on purpose; we'd love to hear whether the nudge feels helpful or naggy.

The shared domain is switched on per server. If Settings or mailgoose account get shows a Shared From, it's on for you; if not, add your own domain for now.

How to test it

  1. Add a subdomain you control, such as news.yourdomain.com, so you don't touch the DNS your main email uses.
  2. Copy each record into your DNS host with the copy buttons, then press Check now (or mailgoose domain verify news.yourdomain.com).
  3. Once it's verified, set your From to an address on it and send yourself a test. In your mail app, look at the message details for SPF, DKIM and DMARC.
  4. If the shared domain is on: add a second address you own as a sender address, click the link in its email, then send a test from it and reply to the test.

What good looks like

  • The domain turns verified within a few minutes of the records going in, and each record says whether it was found.
  • Your test passes SPF and DKIM. A brand-new domain landing in spam at first is normal, and worth telling us about anyway.
  • On the shared domain, the test says it's sending from mailgoose's shared domain with replies to you, and replying reaches your own inbox.

Edge cases worth poking at

  • One record missing or mistyped: does the page tell you which one?
  • A From on a domain you haven't verified (it should be refused with unverified_from and a clear fix).
  • A Reply-To that isn't verified, on the shared domain (refused with unverified_reply_to).
  • A sender address link opened twice, or after it expired (links last 7 days), and an 11th sender address (the limit is 10).

Area 4

The block designer

Where: Emails > New email, and mailgoose email blocks

Build an email from blocks: headings, text, images, buttons, dividers, spacers, two columns and social links. You type headings, text and button labels right on the email, with a small formatting toolbar for bold, italics and links. Drag blocks to reorder them; they snap into the nearest gap. The footer, with the unsubscribe link and your postal address, is added for you. Everything saves as you go, and Save and exit takes you back when you're done.

How to test it

  1. Make a new email and add one of every block type. Upload a picture for the image block, and give it alt text.
  2. Put an image and a button side by side in a columns block.
  3. Use a merge tag in a text block, such as {{first_name|there}} (the part after | is used when a contact has no first name).
  4. Drag blocks around, reload the page, then press Save and exit.
  5. Open the preview, then press Check email and read what it says.
  6. Send a test and open it on your phone and in a desktop mail app.

What good looks like

  • What you see in the designer is what arrives in the inbox, on a phone too.
  • The save state reads "All changes saved" a moment after you stop typing, and nothing is lost on reload.
  • The check lists errors that stop a send (no subject, no postal address, a From you haven't verified) apart from warnings worth a look (such as an image without alt text).

Edge cases worth poking at

  • The same email open in two tabs, edited in both. You should be told about the conflict, never lose an edit silently.
  • Pasting formatted text from a web page or a word processor.
  • A merge tag with a typo, like {{frist_name}}.
  • A huge picture, a non-image file, and a button with no link.
  • Using the designer with only a keyboard, and on a tablet.
  • Going offline for a moment while editing, then coming back.

Area 5

HTML emails

Where: Emails > New email > your own HTML, and mailgoose email import-html

Already have an email you like? Bring the HTML. You edit it in a code editor with a live preview beside it. mailgoose checks the markup and lists every problem with its line. It must include {{unsubscribe_url}} somewhere, so readers can always leave.

How to test it

  1. Paste in an email you've sent before from another tool, or a free template.
  2. Add {{unsubscribe_url}} as a link's address, then send yourself a test.
  3. Duplicate the email from the Emails list and change the copy.

What good looks like

  • The preview keeps up as you type, and the test looks like the preview.
  • Problems point at the right line, in words you can act on.

Edge cases worth poking at

  • HTML without {{unsubscribe_url}}, and HTML with a <script> or a form in it.
  • Broken markup: an unclosed tag, a stray </div>.
  • A very large email, and one with images linked from another site.

Area 6

Contacts and tags

Where: Contacts and Tags, and mailgoose contacts / tags / export

Each account has one list of contacts. You group them with tags and send a campaign to everyone, or to the contacts with certain tags. Each contact is subscribed, unconfirmed (hasn't confirmed by email yet), unsubscribed, bounced or complained, and only subscribed contacts get campaigns.

How to test it

  1. Add a few contacts by hand, with and without names.
  2. Make two tags, put them on different contacts, then rename one and delete the other.
  3. Search the list and filter it by tag and status, then open a contact to see its details and where it came from.
  4. Export your contacts to CSV, and download one contact's data (mailgoose contacts export-data).

What good looks like

  • Tag counts match the contacts you tagged, and a renamed tag stays on its contacts.
  • The export opens cleanly in a spreadsheet, and importing it again gives the same contacts and tags.

Edge cases worth poking at

  • The same address twice, in different capitals, and an address with a typo like gmial.com.
  • Changing an unsubscribed contact back to subscribed. It's refused on purpose: only the person can resubscribe, through a signup form. Tell us how that feels.
  • Deleting a tag that a draft campaign sends to.
  • Your 501st contact (the tester limit is 500).

Area 7

Importing contacts

Where: Contacts > Import, and mailgoose import

Upload a CSV. mailgoose finds the email and name columns from common headers (a Mailchimp audience export works as it is), asks where the people came from, and keeps that as each contact's consent record. Unless you say you have their permission, imported contacts start unconfirmed and get a confirmation email first. Imports that look bought or scraped are held for review.

How to test it

  1. Make a small CSV of addresses you own (or simulator addresses), with an Email Address and a First Name column.
  2. Import it, saying honestly where the people came from, and watch the import's page until it finishes.
  3. Add a tags column and import again.
  4. Try the same from the CLI: mailgoose import contacts.csv --list-source personal --wait.

What good looks like

  • The result counts what was added and lists every skipped row with the reason.
  • Without the permission box ticked, new contacts are unconfirmed and get a confirmation email.

Edge cases worth poking at

  • Semicolons or tabs instead of commas, a file saved from Excel, and accents in names.
  • Bad addresses, repeated rows, addresses already in your list, and an empty file.
  • A file with odd column names, mapped by hand (--map email="E-mail").
  • A file that's not a CSV at all.

Area 8

Signup forms

Where: Signup forms, and mailgoose form

Make a form that asks for an email (and a name, if you like), tags everyone who signs up, and gives you embed code to paste into your site. Double opt-in is on by default: people get an email and are only subscribed once they click to confirm.

How to test it

  1. Make a form with a first name field and a tag. Change the button text and the success message.
  2. Copy the embed code into a page of your own site, or into a blank .html file you open in your browser.
  3. Sign up through it with a second address of yours, then click the link in the confirmation email.
  4. Check the new contact: subscribed, tagged, and its signup recorded.

What good looks like

  • The form fits in with your site, works on a phone, and can be filled in with a keyboard.
  • The contact stays unconfirmed until the button on the confirm page is pressed.

Edge cases worth poking at

  • Signing up twice with the same address, and with an address that's already unsubscribed. The form says the same thing either way, so it can't reveal who's on your list; only one confirmation email goes out per hour.
  • A form with double opt-in turned off, and one that redirects to a thank-you page of yours.
  • Submitting the instant the page loads, or with a required field empty.
  • A form while your account is still in review or in test mode (signups should be refused, or limited to your test addresses).

Area 9

Campaigns

Where: Campaigns, and mailgoose campaign

A campaign is an email, who gets it (everyone subscribed, or contacts with certain tags) and when (now, or a time you pick). Before you can launch, you send a test of the campaign exactly as it is. Launching shows a summary (subject, From, how many people, when, and the credits it uses) and only sends once you confirm.

How to test it

  1. Make a campaign to one tag. Check the recipient count is what you expect.
  2. Send a test to yourself, and to a second address on your verified domain if you have one. The subject starts with [Test].
  3. Press Review and launch, read the summary, and confirm.
  4. Make a second campaign scheduled for 15 minutes from now. Move its send time, then let it go.
  5. Make a third, schedule it, and cancel it before it sends.

What good looks like

  • The summary's numbers match what actually goes out, and the credits used match the copies sent.
  • The scheduled campaign goes at the time you picked, in your time zone.
  • Every copy has a working unsubscribe link and your postal address.

Edge cases worth poking at

  • Change the email after testing, then try to launch. It should ask for a fresh test.
  • A campaign to a tag nobody has, and one bigger than your credits or your 100-a-day cap.
  • A send time in the past, or across a daylight saving change.
  • Two tabs launching the same campaign at once.
  • Deleting the email a draft campaign uses.

Area 10

Reports, bounces and unsubscribes

Where: a sent campaign's Report, and mailgoose campaign report

Each campaign's report shows sent, delivered, bounced, complaints, unsubscribes, opens and clicks, plus clicks per link. Opens are estimates: many mail apps load images on their own, and some never do. A hard bounce or a spam complaint stops all future mail to that address straight away.

To see bounces and complaints without hurting anyone's reputation, use Amazon's mailbox simulator. Add these as contacts, tag them, and send them a campaign. They use no tester credits and are left out of your rates:

AddressWhat it does
success@simulator.amazonses.comDelivered.
bounce@simulator.amazonses.comA hard bounce. The contact becomes bounced.
complaint@simulator.amazonses.comDelivered, then marked as spam. The contact becomes complained.
ooto@simulator.amazonses.comDelivered, with an out-of-office reply.
suppressionlist@simulator.amazonses.comRefused, as if the address were on a suppression list.

How to test it

  1. Send a campaign to yourself and the simulator addresses. Open your copy and click a link in it.
  2. Watch the report fill in over the next few minutes.
  3. Click the unsubscribe link in your copy, then check your contact's status.
  4. Check the simulator contacts' statuses changed as the table says.

What good looks like

  • Every number adds up, and your click shows against the right link.
  • Unsubscribing takes one click, says it worked, and the next campaign skips you.
  • The bounced and complained contacts don't get your next campaign.

Edge cases worth poking at

  • A campaign with open and click tracking turned off (a box when you make the campaign): what does its report show for opens and clicks?
  • Unsubscribing twice, and using your mail app's own unsubscribe button (Gmail and Apple Mail show one).
  • Forwarding your copy to someone and having them unsubscribe.

Area 11

API keys

Where: API keys, and mailgoose keys

Keys let scripts and AI agents use your account. A draft key can do everything except launch or cancel campaigns, change billing and manage keys, which suits an agent that builds and tests while you do the launching. A full key can do everything. A key's secret is shown once.

How to test it

  1. Make a draft key and a full key.
  2. With the draft key, try to launch a campaign from the CLI: it should be refused with insufficient_scope.
  3. Revoke a key, then use it again.

What good looks like

  • A revoked key stops working on its very next request.
  • It's clear, without reading docs, which kind of key to make.

Area 12

The CLI

Where: your terminal, on macOS, Linux or Windows

Every action in the web app has a mailgoose command. Add --json to any of them for output a script or an agent can read. Nothing prompts: anything that sends or deletes waits for an explicit flag.

Install it and point it at staging

There's no installer during the alpha. With Go 1.21 or newer installed (it fetches the newer Go it needs by itself):

If your shell can't find mailgoose afterwards, add Go's bin folder to your PATH (usually ~/go/bin).

Commands to try

What good looks like

  • Whatever you build in the CLI shows up in the web app straight away, and the other way round.
  • Every command's --help has an example you can copy, and errors tell you what to do next.
  • With --json, output is valid JSON and errors go to stderr with a code.

Edge cases worth poking at

  • Commands with a missing argument, a wrong id, and no network.
  • A launch code used twice, a wrong one, and one older than 15 minutes.
  • mailgoose logout, then any command.
  • Exit codes in a script: 0 ok, 1 error, 2 bad usage, 3 not signed in, 4 not found, 5 needs a confirmation (mailgoose help exit-codes).

Area 13

Using it from an AI agent

This is the part we most want feedback on. Any agent that can run commands (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, or your own) can build and send email through the CLI. An agent can do everything up to the launch; a campaign only goes out once you've seen the launch summary and said yes in your chat.

How to test it

  1. Make a draft API key and give it to the agent as environment variables, so it doesn't need to sign in through a browser:
  2. Tell the agent to read the guide written for it: mailgoose docs prints it.
  3. Ask for something real, in plain words. For example: "Build a short email announcing our holiday hours, with a button to our website, and send me a test."
  4. Then try a full key and ask it to launch the campaign to one tag.

What good looks like

  • The agent gets to a good-looking test without you having to explain mailgoose to it.
  • With a draft key, it stops after the test and asks you to launch in the web app.
  • With a full key, it shows you the launch summary and waits for your yes before it confirms.

Edge cases worth poking at

  • Ask it to "just send it" without showing you anything. Tell us if it ever launches without your yes.
  • Edit the email in the web app while the agent is working on it.
  • Ask it to import a list and see whether it asks you where the people came from (it should; it can't guess).

Keep keys out of chat logs you share and out of code you commit. Revoke a key as soon as you're done with it.

Area 14

Credits and billing

Where: Credits and billing, and mailgoose credits / plan

Each email sent uses one credit. The page shows your plan, your balance and its history. You don't need to buy anything to test: your tester credits cover your sends.

Never enter a real card on staging. Buying credits and the paid plan may say billing isn't set up yet, or run in a payment test mode. Either way, no real money should change hands here; if anything asks for a real payment, stop and tell us.

How to test it

  1. Send a test and a small campaign, then check the history shows each one.
  2. Compare mailgoose credits with the page.

What good looks like

  • Every credit used is explained in the history, and copies that failed give theirs back.
  • The tester banner's count goes down as you send.

Reporting a bug

Post it in the #user-feedback channel of the mailgoose team Slack. Your invite includes access to the Slack. One message per bug is easiest for us to follow, and we'll reply in its thread.

A good report has:

  1. Steps to make it happen, starting from which page or command, and which area of this guide it was.
  2. What you expected, and what happened instead.
  3. Your account's email address, so we can find your account.
  4. Where: your browser and device for the web app, or for the CLI the command you ran and what mailgoose version prints.
  5. A screenshot, or the CLI's output with --json. The error's code helps us most.
  6. When it happened, roughly, with your time zone.

Before you send a screenshot or output, cover up any API key, sign-in code or confirmation link. We'll never ask you for them.

Ideas, confusing wording and "I expected this to be here" are just as welcome in #user-feedback as bugs. If something looks like it could expose another account's data or let mail go out without a confirmation, don't post the details in the channel: send them as a direct message to the person who invited you, straight away, and don't try it further.

Please don't

  • Upload real customer lists. Use your own addresses, colleagues who've agreed, and the simulator.
  • Send to anyone who didn't opt in, and no bulk sends, even within your credits. Real complaints on staging hurt delivery for everyone testing with you.
  • Import bought, rented, shared or scraped lists, or addresses copied from somewhere else.
  • Enter a real card or payment details.
  • Send anything you wouldn't send as a real business: no phishing look-alikes, no fake invoices, nothing that pretends to be another company. Our anti-spam policy applies on staging too.
  • Load-test or attack staging. If you'd like to try security or load testing, ask us first and we'll set it up.
  • Share your access or this guide beyond the people we invited.

Thank you for testing. Every report makes mailgoose better for the small businesses and developers it's for.
